How it works

Standard Catan is a fixed hexagon. Pangaea builds free-form continents instead — but free-form shapes make far more coastline than the official board, and coastline quietly wrecks resource balance.

The core problem

Every intersection — where a settlement can go — touches 1, 2 or 3 tiles. A 3-tile spot collects three resources' worth of rolls. Reshape the same tiles into a spikier outline and the tile count doesn't change, but 3-spots turn into 1- and 2-spots.

A
land tiles
S
land-to-land tile edges — how many pairs of tiles touch
v1 v2 v3
intersections touching 1, 2, 3 tiles
Smax
the most S possible for A tiles — a perfectly round blob
L
enclosed lakes (bodies of water, however many hexes)

Euler's formula locks these together:

v3 = S − A + 1 − L
v2 = 3A − S − 3 + 3L
v1 = 3A − S + 3 − 3L      (the generator fills lakes, so L = 0)

So v3 depends only on S. The maximum is Smax = ⌊3A − √(12A − 3)⌋; every edge short of it destroys one 3-spot and creates one 1-spot and one 2-spot.

What the generator does

  1. Grow + compactify — grow a continent, then hill-climb tile positions until S reaches the coastline floor.
  2. Place ports — space port slots evenly along the outer coast.
  3. Anneal — thousands of random swaps of tiles, number tokens and ports, keeping better boards always and worse ones sometimes, less often as the run "cools". That tolerance early on is what stops it getting stuck.
  4. Score — simulate a 1→N→1 snake draft and report how fair it was.

Each candidate is penalised for a 6 or 8 touching another 6/8, identical numbers touching, same-terrain clumps, resource pips out of line with tile counts, 2:1 ports far from (or on top of) their resource, and — most of all — a simulated draft where the best and worst seats end up far apart.

New map builds itself in on every phone at once — the tiles fly in, the numbers flip over, the ports pop on — with sound effects, in step on every phone that has the app open (the build shows where the map's on screen; the sounds play on the tracker too). The volume button in the map's bottom corner mutes the sound effects on your phone (not the music). No sound on an iPhone? Its silent switch mutes them — and any phone needs one tap in the app first.

Game tracker

Tap Tracker during the game. Everyone joins with their profile and a color nobody else has; the game is the map on screen, so New map starts a fresh game for everyone — you keep your seat and color, and season wins are kept. It tracks victory points (13 wins), each player's honor coin (flip your own), knights on the main island and off island, cities, karma, and the robber — Abstain adds 1 to karma and makes the robber worth one more (up to 3), Rob takes its value off karma and starts it over at 1.

WALKER gets a letter each time a 7 is rolled with the barbarians — tap +. As the word fills, the letters shake harder and winter sets in on every phone: pixel snow and frost. + on the full word starts it over and the snow melts away.

On another phone or computer, pick your profile on the welcome screen: if you're in the game you're straight back at the tracker. Your name and Sign out sit next to Pangaea at the top.

Season wins (the trophy at the top) shows the standings and, under them, a calendar: each day shows who won it — tap the day for everyone's points at that win. Swipe the calendar, or use the arrows under it, for other months. The music is out of the way while it's open: on the device playing it, it pauses and plays on when you leave — and a play pressed on another phone meanwhile waits for you to leave, instead of bringing the player back.

Music

Tap ♪ (or the thin music bar along the bottom) to pop the player open: your YouTube or YouTube Music playlist (public or unlisted) with previous / play / next, shuffle and the song list. It stays docked under the map and the tracker — on the right when the phone is sideways — so the phone you cast to the TV shows the board and plays the music. Paste the link once and every phone has it; change it with the 🔗 button. On a phone, start the first song by tapping the video; after that the buttons work. Closing the player pauses the music (YouTube doesn't allow playing from a hidden player) and shrinks it back to the thin bar — its ▶ opens it and plays again, ⏮ ⏭ change the song, the list button shows the songs, and swiping the song sideways goes to the next or previous one.

Everyone shares one player. Every phone shows the same song, in the same order (shuffled or not), playing or paused, at the same time — and anyone's play, pause, next, previous, shuffle, song list or time slider changes it for everyone. The sound comes from one device only: whichever pressed play first, normally the phone cast to the TV — and it stays there whatever anyone changes. On the other phones the video stays still with On Sam's phone under it, and their buttons work as a remote. To move the sound to your phone, tap On Sam's phone → Play here.

On a computer, the music and the tracker share a column on the left of the map — the video sits beside its controls with the song list underneath. Drag the edges to resize the column, the split between them, and the settings on the right.

On a computer, want the tracker and the music without the map? The split-screen icon at the top hides the map: the tracker gets one half and the music the other, with the video across its half and the song list under it. Drag the bar between them to resize; the back arrow brings the map back.

Have YouTube Premium? Under 🔗, Play in the YouTube Music app hands the playlist to the app, which keeps playing in the background with no ads — the bar closes and the map gets the whole screen, and screen mirroring still sends the music to the TV.

Install it as an app

Pangaea installs straight from the browser — no app store — and then opens full screen, without the browser bars. iPhone: in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen (keep Open as Web App on). Android: tap the phone icon at the top, or Install app in the browser menu. The app keeps a copy of itself, so it opens and makes maps with no signal, and it updates itself whenever you're online.

On iPhone the installed app has its own storage, separate from Safari: the first time you open it, pick your profile on the welcome screen and you're straight back in your seat.

Just want full screen without installing? On Android (and iPad) the full-screen icon at the top hides the browser bars. iPhone browsers — Safari, Brave and the rest — don't let a website do that; there, the installed app (added from Safari) is the full-screen version.

Settings

Accent color and Theme (at the bottom of Settings) are yours, not the table's: signed in, your pick is saved with your profile, so the phone, the laptop and the TV all switch to it — change it on one and the others follow a moment later. Signed out, a device keeps its own.
